    What we check

    14 signals AI
    reads first

    1. Structured data (Schema.org)

      A behind-the-scenes label that spells out who you are, what you offer and how to reach you — in a format AI understands perfectly. This is the single biggest win.

    2. Substantial readable text

      AI reads words, not pictures. If your message lives inside images, or only appears after a click, AI simply can't see it.

    3. AI crawlers allowed (robots.txt)

      A small text file that can accidentally tell AI to 'go away'. We make sure it isn't blocking ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity or Google's AI.

    4. llms.txt present

      A simple 'cheat sheet' file made just for AI — it points the AI straight to your most important pages and what your business does. New, but powerful.

    5. Descriptive page title

      The headline AI and Google show for your page. It's the very first thing they read to understand what you do.

    6. Meta description

      The short summary shown under your link in search results and AI answers — think of it as your one-line sales pitch.

    7. Clear heading structure

      These are the 'chapter titles' on your page. They let AI follow your content instead of seeing one big wall of text.

    8. Served over HTTPS

      This is the padlock in the address bar. It keeps your visitors safe, and AI engines trust secure sites far more than unsecured ones.

    9. Social / Open Graph tags

      Controls the little preview card (image + text) shown when your link is shared or surfaced by AI. It makes you look established and trustworthy.

    10. XML sitemap

      A map of all your pages, so AI and Google can find every one — not just your homepage.

    11. Canonical URL

      Tells AI which version of a page is the 'real' one, so you're not marked down for looking like duplicate pages.

    12. Language declared

      Tells AI which language your site is written in, so it reads and quotes you correctly instead of guessing.

    13. Mobile-friendly viewport

      Makes sure your site works properly on phones. Most people — and AI's mobile checks — see the phone version first.

    14. Favicon

      The tiny icon in the browser tab. A small thing, but it signals a real, cared-for website.
